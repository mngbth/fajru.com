1. Interactive Notes 📚

🏪

Business

An economic activity where goods are produced or services are provided and exchanged for money to satisfy human needs and wants and earn profit.

🏢

Business organisation

A legal entity formed by one or more individuals to carry out commercial activities such as producing, buying or selling goods and services to achieve common objectives, usually profit.

🧩

Types

Sole proprietorship, partnership, private limited company, public limited company, franchise, joint venture, public corporation, cooperative, MNC, nationalised organisation and privatised business.

👤

Sole proprietorship

Owned and operated by one person. The owner invests capital, controls the business, receives all profits and is personally responsible for debts and losses.

⚡

Advantages

Simple and inexpensive to set up, quick decisions, owner keeps all profits, privacy, and personal contact can improve customer relationships.

⚠️

Disadvantages

Limited capital and expansion, unlimited liability, personal risk, possible lack of continuity, and limited management expertise.

🤝

Partnership

Owned by two or more people who agree to share profits, responsibilities and liabilities. The agreement is usually stated in a partnership deed.

🧠

Partner types

Active, sleeping (dormant), nominal, secret, and partner by estoppel.

🔎

Key distinction

An active partner manages daily operations; a sleeping partner invests but does not participate in daily management.

🔒

Private limited

Separate legal identity; shareholders have limited liability. Shares are not freely sold to the public and transfer usually needs other shareholders’ consent.

📈

Public limited

Shares can be sold freely to the general public through the stock exchange. It has separate legal identity and limited liability.

🗂️

Company terms

Share, shareholder, dividend, MOA, AOA, COI, BOD, CEO, AGM, voting and stock exchange.

🍔

Franchise

A franchisor permits a franchisee to use its brand, products and business system for regular payment called royalty.

🧪

Joint venture

Two or more organisations cooperate for a specific objective or project, sharing resources, risks and responsibilities while keeping separate identities.

👥

Cooperative

Owned and managed by a group for mutual benefit. Members contribute capital, share responsibilities and participate equally in decision-making.

🌍

MNC

A multi-national company owns and operates branches or subsidiaries in more than one country.

🏛️

Public corporation

Government-owned, established to provide important services to the public, with public welfare as its main objective.

🔄

Nationalised / privatised

Nationalisation transfers a private business to government ownership. Privatisation transfers a government-owned business to private ownership.

🏦

Private sector

Owned and controlled by individuals or private companies; mainly profit-oriented and funded through private investment.

🏛️

Public sector

Owned and controlled by government; provides essential goods and services for public welfare and is funded through government revenue.

⚖️

Compare

Private sector: private ownership, profit, private investment and faster flexible decisions. Public sector: government ownership, public welfare, government revenue and often slower bureaucracy.

💬 Quick memory tip: Private = private owners. Public = government. Nationalisation = government takes over. Privatisation = government transfers to private owners.
